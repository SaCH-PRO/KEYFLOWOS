# Business Genome, Genesis & Command Center

> This is the layer that decides *what kind of business this is* and therefore *what the product is allowed to do for it*. A **Blueprint** is 25 JSON columns of structured business facts on one row per business. A **Genome** is a scored, evidence-backed reading of that Blueprint plus everything the rest of the platform has observed — stored as `GenomeFact` rows, rolled up into section scores, module readiness, department readiness and an overall integrity percentage. **Genesis** is the intake funnel that fills the Blueprint (AI idea extraction, a question bank, compliance inference, projections, a document pack, a market strategy). **Capabilities / readiness gates** are what the score is *for*: `GenomeGateGuard` blocks generative endpoints until the Three-Pillar Minimum is met, and `ModuleReadiness.automationAllowed` tells KEY's autonomy layer whether a module has enough verified facts to act unattended. The **Command Center** is the read model that puts all of it on one screen.

## How it works

The spine is [blueprint.service.ts](apps/server/src/modules/blueprint/blueprint.service.ts). `getBlueprint()` lazily seeds a `BusinessBlueprint` row from the `Business` record on first read, so every other module can assume the row exists. `updateBlueprint()` shallow-merges a patch into each of the 25 section columns, recomputes completeness and confidence, calls `GenomeScoringService.computeFactScores()` and `KeyGenomeService.buildIntegrityResult()`, and writes the derived `genomeIntegrity`, `genomeDnaScores`, `genomeDnaConfidence`, `genomeStage` and `executiveReadinessScore` back onto the same row. It then fires three side effects: a fire-and-forget `KeyGenomeBackfillService.backfill()` scoped to the columns that actually changed, a Temporal Flow event `genome.updated`, and — when integrity moves by at least 1 point — an EventEmitter2 `genome.integrity_changed`.

That last event is caught by [genome-integrity-change.listener.ts](apps/server/src/modules/business-genome/genome-integrity-change.listener.ts), which auto-generates a new `BusinessConstitutionVersion` through [constitution-version.service.ts](apps/server/src/modules/business-genome/constitution-version.service.ts), archives the previous ACTIVE row, and stamps `constitutionVersion` / `constitutionGeneratedAt` back onto the blueprint. So a single field edit anywhere in the product can cascade: blueprint write → fact backfill → rescore → constitution version → Temporal Flow event.

The fact kernel is the actual "genome". [key-genome.ontology.ts](apps/server/src/modules/business-genome/key-genome/key-genome.ontology.ts) is the contract layer: 12 `KeyGenomeSection` keys with weights summing to 100, a `requiredFacts`/`optionalFacts` list per section tagged `BLOCKING | DEGRADED | OPTIONAL`, and a `moduleImpact` list naming which of 14 `KeyGenomeModuleName` values each section feeds. [key-genome-backfill.service.ts](apps/server/src/modules/business-genome/key-genome/key-genome-backfill.service.ts) flattens blueprint JSON into `GenomeFact` rows keyed `(businessId, section, domain, field)`, mapping blueprint column to KeyGenomeSection via its own `BLUEPRINT_SECTION_MAP`. [genome-scoring.service.ts](apps/server/src/modules/business-genome/key-genome/genome-scoring.service.ts) scores every fact on five axes (completeness, quality, confidence, freshness with a 30-day half-life, operational readiness), subtracts a risk penalty, **writes the five sub-scores back to the row**, and rolls facts up per section and then into a weighted business score. This matters because `GenomeFactService.upsertFact()` creates rows with all five sub-scores at 0 — nothing is scored until `computeFactScores()` runs. [genome-decay.service.ts](apps/server/src/modules/business-genome/key-genome/genome-decay.service.ts) runs a 3 AM cron that flips facts idle for over 90 days to `STALE`, which drops their confidence base from 0.7/1.0 to 0.2.

[key-genome.service.ts](apps/server/src/modules/business-genome/key-genome/key-genome.service.ts) is the reconciliation point between the two scoring worlds. It computes a Blueprint-derived field-count score for each of 12 `DnaSectionKey`s, then — via `DNA_TO_KEY_GENOME_SECTION` — overrides any DNA section that has matching KEY Genome facts with the fact-based score. If any facts exist at all, overall `genomeIntegrity` comes from the fact score; if none exist, it falls back to a weighted average of the field counts. It also derives `executiveReadinessScore` from seven weighted DNA sections, `threePillarMinimumMet` (founder ≥ 50, business ≥ 50, market ≥ 50) and a seven-value `GenomeStage` ladder from `CONCEPT` to `ENTERPRISE_READY`.

Gating works two ways. `GenomeGateGuard` ([genome-gate.guard.ts](apps/server/src/core/auth/genome-gate.guard.ts)) recomputes integrity per request and throws `GENOME_GATE_BLOCKED` with the missing pillars unless the Three-Pillar Minimum holds — it is attached to nine generative routes (roadmap, document pack, risk register, market strategy, constitution generation, evolution-proposal generate/approve, genome-chat apply-updates, DNA section patch). Separately, [genome-module-readiness.service.ts](apps/server/src/modules/business-genome/key-genome/genome-module-readiness.service.ts) walks the ontology for each of the 14 modules, fuzzy-matches required facts against actual `GenomeFact` rows, and persists a `GenomeModuleReadiness` row carrying `readinessScore`, `missingFacts`, `blockedReasons` and `automationAllowed` (false whenever any BLOCKING fact is absent). That row is what `key-autonomy` and `key-cortex` read before letting KEY execute unattended, and what [genome-autonomy-gate.service.ts](apps/server/src/modules/business-genome/key-genome/genome-autonomy-gate.service.ts) folds into its ALLOW/APPROVE/BLOCK decision alongside the cross-domain snapshot.

Genesis is the fill side. [business-genesis.service.ts](apps/server/src/modules/business-genesis/business-genesis.service.ts) `analyzeIdea()` sends free text through `ModelGatewayService` under the `genesis_idea_extraction` contract, coerces the result, builds a **non-persistent** preview blueprint, infers Trinidad & Tobago compliance items, scores readiness and returns the next three questions from [genesis-question-bank.ts](apps/server/src/modules/business-genesis/genesis-question-bank.ts). `submitAnswers()` is the persisting path: it calls `blueprint.inferFromOnboarding()`, syncs a few top-level `Business` columns, conditionally recomputes compliance and financial projections, persists, and then writes each answered field as a `GenomeFact` with attached `GenomeEvidence` — using the wrong section vocabulary, see **Wiring reality**. [genome-chat.service.ts](apps/server/src/modules/business-genesis/genome-chat.service.ts) is the conversational variant: the model returns a `proposedUpdates` block, the user confirms, `applyUpdates()` writes the DNA section and mirrors it into facts as `USER_VERIFIED` — and this path *does* translate through `DNA_TO_KEY_GENOME_SECTION` correctly.

Above all of it, [business-command-center.service.ts](apps/server/src/modules/business-command-center/business-command-center.service.ts) `snapshot()` fans out ten calls in parallel behind a `safeResolve` wrapper (every failure degrades to a typed empty value rather than failing the request), then computes fact scores and module readiness *after* the parallel block so readiness sees freshly-scored facts. It converts thirteen different sources — KEY approvals, approved-awaiting-execution, temporal urgencies, module readiness gaps, missing BLOCKING facts, weak genome sections, genome recommendations, genome signals, risks, opportunities, evolution proposals, asset expiries, constitution staleness — into a single `CommandCenterItem[]`, ranks them by priority then by a hardcoded `TYPE_WEIGHT` table, and returns health, pulse, briefing, governance and the top seven priorities. [command-center-key-genome-bridge.service.ts](apps/server/src/modules/business-command-center/command-center-key-genome-bridge.service.ts) adds the cross-domain brief by pulling the latest `GenomeCrossDomainSnapshot`, the ranked recommendations, detected opportunities and any autonomy-gate blocks.

The supporting modules are thinner. [business-event.service.ts](apps/server/src/modules/business-events/business-event.service.ts) is a `@Global()` audit spine whose interceptor is registered as an `APP_INTERCEPTOR` and auto-logs every POST/PUT/PATCH/DELETE that carries a `businessId`, routed through a circuit breaker into a BullMQ queue with an in-memory anomaly detector on the consumer side. [structure.service.ts](apps/server/src/modules/structure/structure.service.ts) owns org units, job roles, assignments and delegation rules, and doubles as the people directory that KEY's chat tools query. [client-momentum.service.ts](apps/server/src/modules/momentum/client-momentum.service.ts) is a contact-level RFM-style scoring engine with its own recommendation table, swept daily by the autopilot module. [capability-contract.service.ts](apps/server/src/modules/capabilities/capability-contract.service.ts) projects the AI `FLOW_TOOLS` registry into a versioned capability contract. [os.service.ts](apps/server/src/modules/os/os.service.ts) is a second, older command-center aggregator that nothing calls.

## Data model

**Exclusively owned by this slice** (zero non-spec readers/writers outside these ten modules, verified per model):

- Fact kernel: `GenomeFact`, `GenomeEvidence`, `GenomeSignal`, `GenomeModuleReadiness`, `GenomeExperiment`, `GenomeDepartment`
- Domain tables: `GenomeFinancialMetric`, `GenomeCustomerSegment`, `GenomeSalesMotion`, `GenomeOperationalProcess`, `GenomeDeliveryCapability`, `GenomeGrowthChannel`, `GenomeContentStrategy`
- Snapshots: `GenomeFinanceSnapshot`, `GenomeCustomerSalesSnapshot`, `GenomeOperationsSnapshot`, `GenomeMarketingSnapshot`, `GenomeCrossDomainSnapshot`
- Learning: `GenomeRecommendationOutcome`, `GenomeOutcomeLearningWindow`
- Narrative/versioning: `GenomeChatMessage`, `BusinessConstitutionVersion`
- Assets & org: `BusinessAsset`, `OrgUnit`
- Genesis output: `MarketStrategy`, `Competitor`

**Written here, read elsewhere:**

- `BusinessBlueprint` — written only by `BlueprintService` and the two constitution writers; read by 3 files outside this slice (`intelligence`, `key-cortex`, `onboarding-concierge`). The genome scores live on this row as denormalized columns (`genomeIntegrity`, `genomeDnaScores`, `genomeDnaConfidence`, `genomeStage`, `executiveReadinessScore`, `constitutionVersion`, `lastGenomeSyncAt`).
- `GenomeRecommendation`, `GenomeMemoryEvent`, `GenomeEvolutionProposal` — also touched by `key-autonomy`/`key-cortex`/`intelligence`.
- `BusinessEvent` — written here, read by 10 files elsewhere; this is the platform audit log.

## Wiring reality

All ten modules are registered in [app.module.ts](apps/server/src/app.module.ts): `BusinessEventModule` (136), `StructureModule` (181), `MomentumModule` (182), `BlueprintModule` (200), `BusinessGenesisModule` (201), `BusinessAssetsModule` (202), `OsModule` (214), `BusinessGenomeModule` (220), `GenomeDocumentPackModule` (226), `BusinessCommandCenterModule` (228), `CapabilitiesModule` (258). `KeyGenomeModule` is reached transitively through `BusinessGenomeModule`, `BlueprintModule`, `BusinessGenesisModule` and `BusinessCommandCenterModule`. Every provider listed in `key-genome.module.ts` is in `providers` and every controller is in a module's `controllers`. The core paths are genuinely live, and the web client's URLs match the controller prefixes route-for-route (checked `business-genome.ts`, `business-genesis.ts`, `business-command-center.ts`, `business-assets.ts`, `business-blueprint.ts`, `structure.ts`, and the momentum block in `client.ts`).

That said, the following are written, registered-looking, and not connected:

**1. `MomentumSchedulerService` is never constructed.** [momentum-scheduler.service.ts:9](apps/server/src/modules/momentum/momentum-scheduler.service.ts) is `@Injectable()` with `OnModuleInit`/`OnModuleDestroy` and a 60-second sweep loop, and it appears in **no** module's `providers`: `grep -rn "MomentumSchedulerService" apps/server/src` returns only two hits, both inside the file itself (the class declaration and its own `Logger` name). Its work is duplicated — and actually performed — by `autopilot/delegation-loop.service.ts:287`, which runs the same timezone-keyed sweep against the same `ClientMomentumService.runDailySweep`. Note that the repo's own dead-provider gate cannot catch this: [unreachable-provider.spec.ts](apps/server/src/core/config/unreachable-provider.spec.ts) short-circuits on `FRAMEWORK_DRIVEN.test(src)`, which matches `OnModuleInit`, and it never checks module registration — so a lifecycle-hook class that is in no `providers` array is silently treated as reachable.

**2. The whole `os` module has no client.** `OsService` has zero injections outside its own directory (`grep -rn "OsService" apps/server/src | grep -v modules/os/` → empty). Its one route is `GET /os/businesses/:businessId/command-center`, and the only caller-shaped thing in the repo is `fetchCommandCenter` in [apps/web/src/lib/api/os.ts](apps/web/src/lib/api/os.ts), which **nothing imports** — `grep -rn "lib/api/os" apps/web/src` returns nothing. The command-center screen at `apps/web/src/app/app/command-center/page.tsx:34` imports `getBusinessCommandCenterSnapshot` from `business-command-center` instead. 273 lines of health scoring that no user has ever seen.

**3. The document-pack export cannot be reached from the browser — twice over.** [business-genome.ts:722-730](apps/web/src/lib/api/business-genome.ts) builds the export URL as a bare path, with no `API_BASE` prefix, unlike every other absolute-URL helper in the web app (compare the expenses export at `client.ts:5584`, which uses `` `${API_BASE}/expenses/...` ``). Both call sites then do `window.open(url, "_blank")` — `executive-brief-panel.tsx:33` and `constitution-panel.tsx:122` — so the browser navigates to the *Next.js* origin, where no such route exists (the API proxy rewrite is `/__api/:path*`, per `next.config.ts:268`). Even with a correct URL it would still fail: the controller is `@UseGuards(AuthGuard, BusinessGuard)`, `req.user` is populated only from an `Authorization: Bearer` header (`auth.middleware.ts:49`), and `window.open` cannot set headers. Separately, the web type union declares `'dna-report'` as an artifact ([business-genome.ts:720](apps/web/src/lib/api/business-genome.ts)) while the server route is `dna/export` — a 404 waiting for the first caller, though no caller uses that value today.

**4. Genesis writes facts under the wrong section vocabulary, so they score as weight 0 and never reconcile.** `BusinessGenesisService.inferGenomeFactTarget` returns blueprint section names — `return { section, domain: section, field, value }` at [business-genesis.service.ts:531](apps/server/src/modules/business-genesis/business-genesis.service.ts) for dotted keys, and `{ section: mapped.section, … }` at line 566 for flat keys, where `mapped.section` is typed `BlueprintSectionKey` (`'identity'`, `'operatingModel'`, …). Those values are then passed straight to `upsertFact` at line 496. But every consumer expects a `KeyGenomeSection` (`'VISION_IDENTITY'`, `'BUSINESS_MODEL'`, …): `GenomeScoringService.computeSectionScores` does `KEY_GENOME_SECTION_WEIGHTS[section] ?? 0`, so a fact sectioned `'identity'` contributes **zero** to the weighted business score; `KeyGenomeService.buildIntegrityResult` looks up `DNA_TO_KEY_GENOME_SECTION[...]` and never finds it; and `GenomeModuleReadinessService.factMatchesRequirement` returns false on the first line (`if (fact.section !== req.section) return false`), so these facts can never satisfy a BLOCKING requirement. The two correct writers do translate: [key-genome-backfill.service.ts:8](apps/server/src/modules/business-genome/key-genome/key-genome-backfill.service.ts) has `BLUEPRINT_SECTION_MAP`, and [genome-chat.service.ts:158](apps/server/src/modules/business-genesis/genome-chat.service.ts) uses `DNA_TO_KEY_GENOME_SECTION`. Because `submitAnswers` calls `blueprint.inferFromOnboarding()` → `updateBlueprint()` → `backfill()` *and* `syncAnswersToGenomeFacts()` on the same answers, each answered field lands twice under two different unique keys — once correctly, once inert. The spec pins the wrong half: `business-genesis.service.spec.ts:82` asserts `section: 'identity'` against a mocked `GenomeFactService`, so nothing in the test can disagree.

**5. `GenomeEvolutionService.buildPatchForSection` returns hardcoded literals.** [genome-evolution.service.ts:254-275](apps/server/src/modules/business-genome/genome-evolution.service.ts) takes `(section, evidence)` and ignores `evidence` entirely, returning `{ channels: ['WhatsApp'] }` for marketing, `{ coreWorkflows: ['appointment-booking'] }` for operations, `{ salesChannels: ['direct-invoice'] }` for sales, `{ operationalRisks: ['Asset expiry reminders detected'] }` for risk, and `{}` for the other eight DNA sections. So `POST evolution-proposals/generate-from-temporal-flow` produces proposals whose `proposedPatch` is a fixed string regardless of what the temporal signal actually said, and approving one writes that literal into the blueprint. Eight of twelve sections silently generate an empty patch.

**6. Orphan public methods on live services.** Each of these has exactly one non-spec occurrence in the tree — its own definition:
- `GenomeSignalService.createFromTemporalEvent` / `createFromInboxThread` / `createFromExecutiveFinding` ([genome-signal.service.ts:290, 326, 361](apps/server/src/modules/business-genome/key-genome/genome-signal.service.ts)) — the three named ingestion adapters. The four modules that do feed signals (`temporal-flow`, `key-inbox`, `device`, `ai/conversation-genome-extractor`) all call the generic `createSignal` instead.
- `KeyGenomeService.recomputeScores` ([key-genome.service.ts:227](apps/server/src/modules/business-genome/key-genome/key-genome.service.ts)) — documented as "recompute the complete genome score ecosystem from a single entry point"; no entry point uses it.
- `OutcomeLearningService.computeLearningSummary` and `computeDomainMemoryConfidenceAdjustment` ([outcome-learning.service.ts:399, 403](apps/server/src/modules/business-genome/key-genome/outcome-learning.service.ts)) — the latter is re-implemented inline as `GenomeRecommendationService.applyMemoryConfidenceAdjustment` (line 598), with the same ±0.05/+0.02 constants but a different clamp and a different query.
- `GenomeMemoryService.countRecentMemoryEvents` / `countRecentFailedOutcomes` ([genome-memory.service.ts:251, 261](apps/server/src/modules/business-genome/key-genome/genome-memory.service.ts)) and `GenomeFactService.countFacts` ([genome-fact.service.ts:213](apps/server/src/modules/business-genome/key-genome/genome-fact.service.ts)).

**7. `CapabilityContractService` has no in-process consumer.** `grep -rn "CapabilityContractService\|CapabilitiesModule" apps/server/src | grep -v capabilities/` returns exactly one line — the `app.module.ts:258` import. The service is `exports:`-ed but nothing injects it, and no web file references `/capabilities`. The three HTTP routes are genuinely mounted, so this is *reachable* rather than dead, but the docstring's premise ("how UI buttons, automations, external apps and KEY itself learn which business actions exist") has no implementation on the consumer side yet.

**8. Cosmetic but pervasive: 17 duplicated `@Inject(forwardRef(...))` decorator pairs.** The same decorator is applied twice to the same constructor parameter across 8 files — 4 in [genome-cross-domain.service.ts:71-74](apps/server/src/modules/business-genome/key-genome/genome-cross-domain.service.ts), 4 in `operations-genome.service.ts`, 2 each in `finance-genome`, `customer-sales-genome`, `marketing-genome`, and 1 each in `department-readiness`, `genome-recommendation`, `genome-recommendation-outcome`. Both copies resolve the same token, so behaviour is unchanged, but it reads as a botched codemod.

Not defects, worth knowing: `GenomeDecayService` is in `providers` but deliberately absent from `exports` — its `@Cron` is its only trigger, which is correct. `GenomeFactService.upsertFact` writes zeros for four of five sub-scores, so a fact is genuinely unscored until some caller runs `computeFactScores()`; the Command Center and `updateBlueprint` both do this, but a fact created by a signal merge and then read directly via `listFacts` carries stale sub-scores. All ten `@Body()` spreads in `key-genome.controller.ts` put the guarded `businessId` **last**, and the one that used to be exploitable (`recommendations/generate`) now carries an explicit comment explaining why the ordering is load-bearing.

## Open questions

1. **Is the Genesis fact-section mismatch (#4) a regression or was it always wrong?** The blueprint/chat writers both translate correctly and were clearly written with the two vocabularies in mind; only Genesis skipped it. Fixing it means either deleting `syncAnswersToGenomeFacts` (the backfill already covers the same fields) or routing it through `BLUEPRINT_SECTION_MAP` — and either choice orphans the existing inert rows, which nothing currently cleans up.
2. **Which command center is canonical?** `os` and `business-command-center` both aggregate business health from overlapping tables with different scoring. `os` is unreferenced today, but nothing marks it deprecated.
3. **`OsService.computeHealthScore(cashBalance, overdueInvoices, billsDue)` feeds raw currency amounts into a 0-100 clamp** — a business with any cash at all scores 100 on money, and a large overdue balance is added as a "penalty" that is itself a currency amount. I could not tell from the code whether this is intentional or vestigial, and there is no test.
4. **What sets `GenomeFact.riskIfWrong`?** Every create path hardcodes `'MEDIUM'` ([genome-fact.service.ts:154](apps/server/src/modules/business-genome/key-genome/genome-fact.service.ts)) and no update path changes it, yet `KEY_GENOME_RISK_PENALTIES` defines four tiers and the scoring formula subtracts from them. The CRITICAL/HIGH/LOW penalties are unreachable in practice.
5. **Is `automationAllowed` enforced anywhere it matters?** `key-autonomy` and `key-cortex` read `GenomeModuleReadiness`, but I did not trace whether a `false` value actually blocks execution or merely annotates a proposal — that lives in the autonomy slice.
6. **Does the fuzzy `factMatchesRequirement` matcher over-match?** It accepts bidirectional `includes()` on both domain and field after stripping separators, so a required `finance.currency` could be satisfied by a fact at `financials.currencyCode`. That may be deliberate (it is what makes backfilled `field: 'value'` rows work), but it also means readiness can report a requirement met by an unrelated field.
7. **Why does `BusinessAssetsController` invert the prefix** to `businesses/:businessId/business-assets` while every sibling uses `<module>/businesses/:businessId`? It works and the web client matches, but it is the only one.
