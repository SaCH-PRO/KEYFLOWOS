I think this feedback is very good. It is practical, product-aware, and better scoped than a purely aspirational response.

What I like most is that it does 3 useful things at once:
it validates the scenario,
it anchors the discussion in what already exists,
and it cleanly identifies the gap between current capabilities and the vision. That makes it much more actionable than a generic “yes this is a good idea” reply.

The strongest parts

It correctly says the scenario is real and important. Darius’ messages are exactly the kind of messy, overlapping customer intent that a normal service catalog cannot handle. The feedback is right that the app should convert fragmented intent into structured execution, not just list offers.

It also does a very important product-management move: it separates “already exists,” “already planned,” and “still missing.” That prevents duplication and keeps the roadmap honest. The distinction it makes between current store/catalog capability, ongoing graph/action foundation work, and the missing guided qualification layer is especially strong 

Its prioritization is also smart:
build the guided store experience and execution models next,
leave external app ingestion for later,
because external integrations depend on the internal event and action foundation being solid first. That sequencing makes sense.

What I think it gets especially right

The most important insight in the feedback is this:
the missing piece is not “more AI,” it is structured decision support in the store.

That is a very strong diagnosis.

Because your real customer problem is not:
“how do I show more products?”

It is:
“how do I help confused, budget-sensitive customers choose the right engagement model and move into execution?”

The feedback correctly identifies that the current store likely functions more like a catalog than a qualifier, and that the missing layer is the guided package selector:
need -> budget -> urgency -> execution preference -> best-fit offer.

That is probably the single most useful next product move.

The second very strong point is the idea of execution model tiers:

* DIY
* AI-assisted
* hybrid
* full-service

That is excellent product thinking.
It is much closer to how real service buyers think than ordinary package labels.
And for your Darius example, it is probably the core differentiator.

What I would add

I would say the feedback is about 85–90% complete, but I would add two things.

First, I would make “quote-from-conversation” a named gap.
The feedback talks about qualification flow and execution models, which is right, but your Darius example also clearly shows a quoting problem:
the customer is half-asking for scope, half-asking for price, half-negotiating against their own budget.
KeyFlow should be able to turn that into a structured draft offer or quote very quickly.

Second, I would add “asset intake + execution kickoff” as part of the same flow.
Because in your scenario, the store experience should not end at package selection.
Once the customer chooses a path, the next system behavior should be:

* collect logo/tagline/assets
* define output style
* create folder/template workspace
* start content workflow
* schedule follow-up/approval steps

Without that, the store may qualify well but still fail to transition smoothly into delivery.

Where I agree strongly with the recommendation

I strongly agree that external app integrations should not be the immediate next build unless the internal graph/action work is already stable.

That is a classic trap:
teams get excited about WhatsApp, Canva, Meta, external forms, and connectors before the core business graph and action model are strong enough to normalize those inputs properly.

So yes:
guided store experience first,
execution model support second,
external ingestion after the internal nervous system is trustworthy.

My verdict

This is strong feedback.
It is grounded, not fluffy.
It identifies the right next-layer missing from the roadmap.
And it prioritizes in the right order.

If I were using it operationally, I would accept it with one small upgrade:
expand the identified gap from 3 items to 5 items:

* guided package selector
* execution model tiers
* quote-from-conversation
* asset intake + kickoff workflow
* external app ingestion later

That would make it even more complete.

Overall: very solid feedback, and I think it improves the direction rather than just commenting on it.
