Yes — the cleanest way to structure it is:

**Core = documents every operating business should keep from day one**
**Optional = documents generated only when a trigger exists**

There is no single legal checklist that is identical in every country, because registration, tax IDs, permits, insurance, labor records, and recurring filings depend on the business structure, location, and headcount. But every real business does need a baseline entity file plus records that clearly show income, expenses, assets, and tax position. ([Small Business Administration][1])

## Core needed for all businesses

### 1. Company identity pack

This is the “who are we” file.

* Legal business name and trading names
* Registration / formation certificate or equivalent
* Governing document

  * LLC operating agreement
  * company bylaws / articles
  * partnership agreement
  * sole proprietor registration record, if applicable
* Registered address and principal address record
* Owner / founder register
* Director / manager / authorized signatory list
* Tax ID / EIN / business tax registration record
* License and permit register

  * even if the entry is “none currently required”
* Bank account and signing authority record

### 2. Core finance and tax pack

This is the “prove the money” file.

* Chart of accounts
* Bookkeeping ledger / accounting file
* Bank statements archive
* Bank reconciliation file
* Customer invoice template
* Supplier bill / expense record template
* Receipt and proof-of-payment archive
* Fixed asset register, if any assets exist
* Tax calendar
* Tax filings archive
* Year-end financial statements or management accounts

  * at minimum: profit and loss, balance sheet, cash flow view

The IRS says businesses should keep records that support income, expenses, assets, employment taxes, and items reported on returns, and that the recordkeeping system must clearly show income and expenses. ([IRS][2])

### 3. Core commercial pack

This is the “how we sell and get paid” file.

* Standard proposal / quotation template
* Standard customer agreement or terms of business
* Statement of work / scope template
* Pricing sheet / rate card
* Payment terms
* Refund / cancellation terms
* Credit control / collections wording
* Client onboarding form
* Invoice template
* Client acceptance / signoff form

### 4. Core brand and messaging pack

This is the “how we present ourselves” file.

* One-line company description
* Short company bio
* Long company profile
* Founder bio template
* Mission / vision / positioning statement
* Elevator pitch
* Boilerplate “About the company” paragraph
* Tagline / slogan bank
* Key messaging pillars
* Tone of voice guide
* Basic brand asset sheet

  * name, logo, colors, fonts, usage notes

This belongs in the core stack because every business communicates publicly somewhere — sales deck, website, proposal, invoice footer, email signature, directory listing, or social profile. Also, slogans can function as trademarks, so your system should treat taglines as controlled assets, not throwaway copy. ([USPTO][3])

### 5. Core operations pack

This is the “how we actually run” file.

* Master business profile
* Offer / service catalog
* SOP for delivering the main product or service
* Approval matrix / delegation of authority
* Signature authority rules
* Issue / risk log
* Change log
* Master document register
* File naming and versioning standard
* Renewal / review calendar

### 6. Core recordkeeping and audit pack

This is the “show what changed, when, and why” file.

* Document version history
* Approval log
* Source-of-truth field registry
* Evidence folder structure
* Renewal and expiry tracker
* Impact map

  * which profile fields affect which documents
* Archive policy
* Retention schedule

That last item is critical for your product idea. The system should not just generate; it should preserve what facts were used, which template version was used, who edited it, and whether the document is now stale.

## Optional / conditional documents

These are not needed by every business on day one. They become needed when a trigger exists.

### A. People / HR module

**Trigger:** first employee, payroll setup, or formal hiring

* Offer letters
* Employment agreements
* Job descriptions
* Employee handbook
* Payroll setup records
* Confidentiality and inventions agreement
* Performance review forms
* Disciplinary forms
* Leave / time-off policies
* Offboarding checklist
* Personnel file checklist
* Workplace poster pack
* Health and safety records
* Required hiring verification forms

In the U.S., every employer must complete Form I-9 for each employee, EEOC rules require retention of personnel and employment records, and many employers with more than 10 employees must keep OSHA injury and illness records unless exempt by industry. ([USCIS][4])

### B. Contractor / freelancer module

**Trigger:** first contractor, consultant, agency, or creator

* Independent contractor agreement
* Contractor SOW
* Deliverables acceptance form
* IP assignment or license terms
* Confidentiality agreement
* Contractor onboarding packet
* Contractor payment / invoicing terms
* Tax form collection workflow, where applicable

### C. Privacy / data / cybersecurity module

**Trigger:** any personal data, website forms, mailing list, analytics, app, SaaS, employee data, payment data

* Privacy policy / privacy notice
* Internal data handling policy
* Data inventory / data map
* Data retention and deletion schedule
* Cookie notice / consent language
* Data processing agreement
* Vendor / subprocessor register
* Access control policy
* Incident response plan
* Security policy
* Breach response checklist
* Data subject request workflow

FTC guidance says many businesses keep sensitive personal information about customers or employees and should collect only what they need, keep it secure, and dispose of it safely; NIST’s small business cybersecurity guidance is designed specifically to help smaller organizations stand up a risk-management baseline. ([Federal Trade Commission][5])

### D. Website / app / e-commerce module

**Trigger:** website, app, checkout flow, membership portal, marketplace, or digital product

* Website terms of use
* Checkout terms
* Returns policy
* Shipping policy
* Subscription terms
* Acceptable use policy
* Cookie policy
* Community guidelines
* Affiliate / referral terms
* DMCA / takedown process, if relevant
* Support and refund macros

### E. Founder / ownership / investor module

**Trigger:** multiple founders, equity split, fundraising, vesting, board oversight

* Founder agreement
* Shareholders’ agreement
* Operating agreement with ownership economics
* Cap table
* Equity vesting documents
* Stock / unit issuance docs
* Board or manager resolutions
* Written consents
* Minutes
* SAFE / convertible note docs
* Investor update template
* Due diligence data room index

A useful live note for a U.S.-focused system: FinCEN changed BOI reporting in 2025, and U.S.-created entities are currently exempt from BOI reporting, while foreign reporting companies may still have filing obligations. That is exactly why jurisdiction-sensitive filing modules should be rule-based and updated centrally. ([FinCEN.gov][6])

### F. Vendor / procurement module

**Trigger:** suppliers, outsourced services, software vendors, distributors

* Vendor agreement
* Purchase order template
* Vendor onboarding form
* Supplier code of conduct
* Vendor risk questionnaire
* NDA
* Service levels / SLA
* Renewal tracker
* Certificate of insurance request template
* Approved vendor register

### G. Facilities / asset module

**Trigger:** office, store, warehouse, vehicles, machinery, leased assets

* Lease
* Equipment lease / finance agreement
* Asset register
* Maintenance logs
* Site safety procedures
* Visitor policy
* Incident report form
* Insurance certificates
* Property handover checklist

### H. Brand / IP protection module

**Trigger:** strong brand, public campaign, product line, franchising, licensing, expansion

* Trademark search file
* Trademark application file
* Trademark register
* Brand guidelines
* Logo usage policy
* Copyright ownership register
* Content license templates
* Photography / media release forms
* Brand partnership agreement
* Press kit / media kit

USPTO guidance makes clear that trademarks can include words, designs, and slogans used to identify the source of goods or services, so slogans, taglines, and product names should sit inside a controlled IP workflow. ([USPTO][3])

### I. Insurance / risk module

**Trigger:** employees, premises, contracts requiring coverage, valuable assets, professional exposure

* Insurance schedule
* Policy archive
* Certificates of insurance
* Claims log
* Incident reporting forms
* Business continuity plan
* Disaster recovery checklist

SBA notes that insurance requirements vary by state, and businesses with employees often have mandatory coverage obligations. ([Small Business Administration][7])

### J. Manufacturing / inventory / quality module

**Trigger:** inventory, physical goods, food, cosmetics, devices, production, fulfillment

* Purchase orders
* Supplier specs
* QC checklist
* Batch / lot records
* Inventory count sheets
* Recall procedure
* Warranty policy
* Returns inspection form
* Packaging and labeling approval records

### K. Regulated industry module

**Trigger:** healthcare, finance, payments, education, construction, food, childcare, transportation, government contracting, etc.

* Compliance manual
* Mandatory disclosures
* Industry-specific consent forms
* Regulatory register
* Inspection logs
* Incident escalation procedures
* Internal audit plan
* Complaint handling procedure
* Training records
* Required notices and filings

### L. International / multi-jurisdiction module

**Trigger:** selling, hiring, warehousing, or registering in another country/state

* Local registration file
* Local tax registration file
* Cross-border contract variants
* Local privacy addenda
* Import / export records
* Local employment packs
* Foreign vendor onboarding pack
* Jurisdiction matrix
* Translation-controlled templates

## The simplest way to model this in your system

Use three labels only:

**1. Universal core**
Every business gets these automatically.

**2. Triggered core**
Required once the business has a website, workers, contractors, investors, physical premises, or personal data.

**3. Advanced / optional**
Useful, but only for scale, regulation, fundraising, or expansion.

That will keep the product clean and stop it from overwhelming the user with 200 documents on day one.

## If I were building your template hierarchy, I’d define the baseline stack like this

**Universal core stack**

* Identity
* Finance/tax
* Commercial
* Brand/messaging
* Operations
* Audit/version control

**Triggered core stack**

* HR
* Contractors
* Privacy/security
* Website/e-commerce
* Vendors
* Insurance
* IP/trademark
* Governance/investor
* Facilities
* Inventory/quality
* Regulatory
* International

That structure is strong enough for your product architecture and simple enough for onboarding.

The best next move is to turn this into a **master document taxonomy** with columns like: `document name`, `category`, `core/optional`, `trigger`, `risk tier`, `required profile fields`, `reviewer`, and `renewal cycle`.
